Editorial Standards / 001

Evidence first.
Unknowns visible.

HUMAN is written for curiosity without treating uncertainty as a nuisance. We separate what a study directly shows from the mechanisms we infer and the questions that remain open.

01 / Evidence

Direct findings come first.

Claims are anchored to primary studies, quantitative reviews and high-quality syntheses whenever possible. Evidence strength is shown explicitly when it materially affects interpretation.

02 / Mechanism

Mechanisms are not facts by typography.

A plausible mechanistic synthesis is labeled as synthesis. We do not turn fMRI activation, dopamine language or computational metaphors into stronger causal claims than the underlying study supports.

03 / Figures

Conceptual figures stay conceptual.

Illustrative diagrams are marked as conceptual and not empirical data. Effect sizes, causal arrows and measured quantities are not invented for visual drama.

04 / Uncertainty

Unknown is an editorial category.

Where evidence is indirect, observational, culturally narrow or simply missing, that limitation is shown instead of being hidden in a footnote.

05 / Culture

Pop culture is a lens, not a diagnosis.

Named celebrities and fan communities are not clinically diagnosed from afar. Cultural examples are used to open broader questions about ordinary human mechanisms.

06 / Corrections

Claims can change.

Articles carry update dates. When important evidence changes the interpretation, HUMAN should revise the article rather than preserve an outdated claim for consistency.
